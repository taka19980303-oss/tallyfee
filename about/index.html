About this suite — and how every number is verified

The Fee Calculator Suite is a set of free calculators for the money small sellers and small businesses actually pay: marketplace selling fees, Amazon KDP royalties and printing costs, and the real total cost of email and CRM software. Every figure on every page was read on the platform's or vendor's official page — and each page carries the date it was checked.

What is here

Ten marketplace fee calculators (eBay, Etsy, Poshmark, Mercari, Amazon, Depop, Vinted, Whatnot, TikTok Shop and Facebook Marketplace) with a batch calculator, a reverse-pricing tool and an eight-platform comparison; the KDP cover-size, printing-cost and royalty calculators, including a downloadable full-size cover template; and software pricing for six email tools and five CRMs, with cost calculators that price the plan you would actually land in — not the headline tier.

The verification method

Official pages only. Marketplace rates come from the platforms' own fee and help pages, KDP figures from Amazon's official KDP help pages, and software prices from the vendors' own pricing pages. Blog posts, affiliate roundups and other calculators are never used as sources.

A dated check on every page. Each page states when its rates were last verified: the marketplace rates on November 18, 2026 (TikTok Shop and Facebook Marketplace on November 23, 2026), the software prices on November 23, 2026. If a page's date is old, treat its numbers as stale — that is what the date is for.

Promotional prices are never the list price. Intro discounts, first-year offers and coupon rates are ignored; the calculators use the standard published price you would pay on renewal.

Numbers are never invented. Where a vendor does not publish a figure, the calculator says so instead of estimating one. ActiveCampaign publishes no monthly-billing prices and Mailchimp publishes no annual rate card, so those rows show a note, not a borrowed number. Zoho publishes USD annual rates only.

Rounding you can re-perform. Every fee component is rounded to the nearest cent first, and every total is the sum of the already-rounded components — so a breakdown on this site always adds up to its total, to the cent, and you can check it with a pocket calculator.

Two worked examples of the rule

Mailchimp's pages disagreed with each other. When the software prices were checked on November 23, 2026, Mailchimp's own pages showed two different starting prices for the Premium plan ($350 on one page, $236.52 on another). The calculator uses the figure from the pricing page itself, and the discrepancy is disclosed on the Mailchimp pricing page rather than quietly resolved.

monday CRM has no confirmable USD price. monday prices by billing region, and its official page serves yen pricing from Japan. Rather than converting at a guessed exchange rate, monday is documented in yen on its pricing page and left out of the USD CRM calculator entirely.

Corrections

Rates change, and when they do, three things are updated together: the calculator, the page's verification date, and the fee-change history — the public log of every change behind these calculators, each entry dated and linked to its source. You can follow new entries by RSS. If a change cannot be sourced, it is not listed and the calculators do not use it.

Your numbers stay yours

Every calculation runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent to a server. Calculator pages may remember your last inputs in your own browser and mirror them into the page address, so a link you copy pre-fills the same numbers for whoever opens it — that is the only place your inputs ever go.
