KDP Royalties Explained (2026): Every Rate, With Worked Examples

The short answer: print books pay (royalty rate × list price) − printing cost, where the rate is 60% at or above $9.99 on Amazon.com and 50% below it (40% through Expanded Distribution). eBooks pay 70% of the price after VAT and delivery costs inside the $2.99–$12.99 band, and 35% outside it. Every figure below is checked against KDP’s official Help pages, and the royalty calculator runs the same formulas.

Print royalties: the rate depends on your price

Until June 2025, KDP print royalties were a flat 60% of list price minus printing cost. They are now tiered by list price in every marketplace. The thresholds — the list price at or above which the 60% rate applies — are:

The tier applies to the whole price, not as a blend — which creates a cliff at the threshold. KDP’s own worked example makes the mechanics plain: a 333-page black-ink paperback at a $15 list price has a printing cost of $5.00, so the royalty is (0.60 × $15) − $5.00 = $4.00 on Amazon, and (0.40 × $15) − $5.00 = $1.00 through Expanded Distribution. Hardcovers use the same formula and tiers, with higher printing costs and no Expanded Distribution.

Printing costs: fixed plus per page, with a flat band

Printing cost = fixed cost + (page count × per-page cost), set by ink, paper, trim class and marketplace. Short books skip the per-page part entirely. Amazon.com, regular trim:

A trim wider than 6.12 in or taller than 9 in pays the large-trim rate instead (for example $2.84 flat for a short black-ink paperback). KDP sets a minimum list price — printing cost ÷ royalty rate — so a royalty can never go negative; which rate divides depends on which tier the resulting price falls into.

eBook royalties: 70% inside the band, 35% outside

The 70% option pays 70% × (list price − VAT − delivery cost). Delivery is charged per megabyte of file size: $0.15/MB on Amazon.com, £0.10 on Amazon.co.uk, €0.12 in the euro stores, ¥1 on Amazon.co.jp (waived there for files of 10 MB or more), with a $0.01 minimum. The 35% option pays 35% × (list price − VAT) with no delivery cost.

The 70% option requires the list price to sit inside the store’s band. On Amazon.com the band is $2.99–$12.99 since July 7, 2026 — the upper limit was $9.99 from 2007 until that change. Other stores: £1.77–£12.99 (UK), €2.69–€12.99 (EU), C$2.99–C$12.99 (Canada), A$3.99–A$15.99 (Australia), ¥250–¥1,650 (Japan). In Brazil, Japan, Mexico and India, the 70% option additionally requires KDP Select enrollment. The 70% price must also be at least 20% below any print edition’s list price, and public-domain titles are limited to 35%.

Worked example. A $4.99 eBook with a 2 MB file on Amazon.com: delivery = 2 × $0.15 = $0.30, royalty = 0.70 × ($4.99 − $0.30) = $3.28. At the 35% option the same book pays 0.35 × $4.99 = $1.75 — the band is worth $1.53 on every sale.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage does KDP pay authors in 2026?

Print: 60% of list price minus printing cost at or above the marketplace threshold ($9.99 on Amazon.com), 50% below it, 40% through Expanded Distribution. eBooks: 70% of the price after VAT and delivery costs inside the store’s price band, otherwise 35% after VAT.

When did the print royalty tiers start?

June 2025. Before that, print royalties were a flat 60% at any price. Calculators and articles written before the change still show the flat rate and overstate what a sub-$9.99 book earns.

Why is the eBook band $12.99 now?

KDP widened it effective July 7, 2026, raising the 70% option’s maximum list price on Amazon.com from $9.99 to $12.99 — the first change to that cap since 2007 — with equivalent changes in the other Kindle stores.

How do I raise my royalty without raising my price?

Cut the printing cost: fewer pages (past the flat band, each black-ink page costs $0.012 per copy on Amazon.com), black ink instead of color, groundwood instead of white/cream paper, or a regular instead of large trim. Or cross the threshold: moving from $9.98 to $9.99 raises the rate itself from 50% to 60%.
