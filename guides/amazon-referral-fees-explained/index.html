Amazon Referral Fees Explained (2026): Category Rates, Brackets and Minimums

The short answer: Amazon’s referral fee is set by category — 15% for most everyday categories, 8% for Consumer Electronics and Computers, and tiered schedules for Clothing, Jewelry and Watches. It is charged on the total sales price (item + shipping + gift wrap), or the category minimum — $0.30 in most categories — whichever is greater. On a $55 total in Home & Kitchen, the fee is $8.25. Per Amazon’s official pricing page: “For every item sold, sellers pay Amazon a percentage of the total price, or a minimum amount, whichever is greater.” The total sales price is the item price plus the shipping you charge and any gift-wrap charges. Selling-plan fees and FBA fees are separate — see the misunderstandings below.

Worked example 1 — Home & Kitchen, $55 total

An item priced at $50 with $5 of shipping charged to the buyer: the total sales price is $55. Home & Kitchen charges 15%, so the referral fee is $55 × 15% = $8.25 — above the $0.30 minimum, so the minimum does not bind. The minimum matters at the other end: on a $1.50 total, 15% would be $0.23, so Amazon charges the $0.30 minimum instead. Run either case in the calculator and the same figures appear.

Worked example 2 — Jewelry tiers vs Clothing brackets

Amazon runs two different kinds of tier, and mixing them up is the most common referral-fee error. Portion categories charge each rate only on its slice: Jewelry at a $300 total pays 20% on the first $250 = $50.00, plus 5% on the remaining $50 = $2.50 — a total of $52.50. Bracket categories set one rate by the total and apply it to the whole amount: Clothing at a $25 total falls in the over-$20 bracket, so the fee is 17% of the full $25 = $4.25 — not 5% / 10% / 17% sliced up. The calculator models each category the way Amazon’s table defines it.

Common misunderstandings

“Amazon charges 15% on everything.” The category decides: Consumer Electronics and Computers are 8%, Automotive & Powersports 12%, Jewelry up to 20% — and a product’s fee category may not match the category shoppers see in the store.

“The fee is charged on the item price.” It is charged on the total sales price — item price + the shipping you charge + gift-wrap charges. Charging $5 for shipping adds $5 to the fee base.

“All the tiered categories work the same way.” They do not. Bracket categories (Clothing, Beauty, Baby, Grocery) apply a single rate to the whole total; portion categories (Jewelry, Watches, Electronics Accessories) apply each rate only to its portion. Worked example 2 shows both.

“The referral fee is the whole cost of selling on Amazon.” It is one layer. Selling-plan fees (Individual $0.99 per item, Professional $39.99 per month), FBA fulfilment and storage fees, and closing fees other than Media’s $1.80 sit outside it — deliberately out of scope for this guide and the calculator, because they depend on your plan and fulfilment choices.

Frequently asked questions

What is Amazon’s referral fee?

A category-based percentage of the total sales price (item price + shipping + gift wrap), or a minimum amount ($0.30 in most categories), whichever is greater. Most everyday categories are 15%; Consumer Electronics and Computers are 8%; Jewelry is tiered at 20% up to $250 and 5% above.

Does Amazon charge the referral fee on shipping?

Yes — the base is the total sales price, which includes the shipping you charge and any gift-wrap charges.

How is Clothing’s referral fee calculated?

By bracket: the rate is set by the total sales price — 5% at $15 or less, 10% above $15 up to $20, 17% above $20 — and that single rate applies to the whole total, unlike Jewelry or Watches where each rate applies only to its portion.

What is not included in the referral fee?

Selling-plan fees ($0.99 per item on Individual, $39.99 per month on Professional), FBA fulfilment and storage fees, and closing fees other than Media’s $1.80 per item. Those depend on your plan and fulfilment choices, so they are deliberately out of scope here.
