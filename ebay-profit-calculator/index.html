How the reverse calculation works

The solver searches the item price using eBay’s own fee function — 13.6% on the first $7,500 of the total amount of the sale, 2.35% above, plus the $0.30/$0.40 per-order fee — with every fee component rounded to the cent, exactly as in the eBay fee calculator. Because the fee schedule is tiered, the required price is not a simple gross-up: above $7,500 the marginal fee drops to 2.35%, so each extra dollar of price keeps more. Assumptions: no sales tax, no Promoted Listings, no Top Rated Plus discount, US seller location — change those in the main calculator if they apply to you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I price on eBay to make a $50 profit?

Enter your item cost, your shipping cost and what you charge the buyer for shipping, set the target to $50, and the calculator returns the list price. With $40 cost, $8 shipping cost and $10 shipping charged, the answer is $103.89.

Why isn’t the required price just cost ÷ (1 − 13.6%)?

Because eBay’s fee is tiered (2.35% above $7,500), the per-order fee is fixed, and fees are charged on price + shipping + tax — not on the item price alone. A flat gross-up misprices in both directions.

Does this work for a target margin instead?

Yes — switch to “Target margin %” and enter the margin you want as a percentage of revenue (price + shipping charged).
