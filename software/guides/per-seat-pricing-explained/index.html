Per-seat pricing, explained: the 5 ways the seat price isn't the price

Every CRM in our comparison advertises a per-seat price. Here is what that number leaves out — each item taken from a real pricing page we verified on November 23, 2026.

✓ Prices verifiedNovember 23, 2026 — every price on this page was read on the vendor's official pricing page. Promotional prices are never used as the list price.

1. The annual discount is real money

Annual billing cuts the per-seat price by 17–42% across the five CRMs: Pipedrive Lite drops from $24 to $14 a seat, HubSpot Starter from $15 to $9. On a 10-seat team that is the difference between $2,880 and $1,680 a year for the same plan. The catch is commitment — you pay the year up front.

2. Mandatory onboarding fees

HubSpot charges a one-time onboarding fee of $1,500 on Professional and $3,500 on Enterprise, and it is not optional. For a 3-seat Professional team the fee adds 46% to the first year's seat cost. Most “HubSpot pricing” roundups never mention it; our calculator includes it in year one.

3. Seat types and seat minimums

HubSpot sells two kinds of seats — full Sales Seats and cheaper Core Seats ($50–$75) — so the real bill depends on the mix of your team, not the headcount. monday CRM starts every plan at a 3-seat minimum. Close's cheapest plan, Solo, is capped at a single user: add a second seat and you are on a plan that costs four times as much per seat.

4. Usage billing on top of seats

Close charges for calling and SMS by usage (about $0.02/minute outbound) on top of the seat price. Freshsales meters its AI agent by the session ($49 per 100 sessions). For a team that lives on the phone or leans on AI features, the meter can rival the seats.

5. The bundle trap

Zoho One's headline price looks unbeatable until you read the licensing model: “All Employee Pricing” means a license for every employee on payroll, not just the people who use the CRM. A 30-person company with a 4-person sales team pays for 30.

The fix: always compare year-one totals

Per-seat-per-month is the one number designed to look small. Compare instead on year-one total for your actual team size — seats × price × 12, plus one-time fees — which is exactly what the CRM cost calculator computes.
