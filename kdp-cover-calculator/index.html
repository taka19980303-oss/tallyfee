How KDP sizes a paperback cover

Specs verified: November 23, 2026 on KDP’s official Help · Cover width = bleed + back cover + spine + front cover + bleed · Cover height = bleed + trim height + bleed · Bleed is 0.125 in on the top, bottom and outside edges.

The spine is a formula, not a guess. KDP multiplies your page count by a per-page thickness that depends on the interior paper. Change the page count or the paper and the spine — and therefore the whole cover file — changes size. That is why a cover built for a 200-page manuscript gets rejected when the interior ships at 214 pages.

Worked example. A 6 × 9 in paperback, 200 pages, black ink on white paper: spine = 200 × 0.002252 = 0.450 in; full cover = 0.125 + 6 + 0.450 + 6 + 0.125 = 12.700 × 9.250 in — 3,810 × 2,775 px at the 300 DPI KDP requires. The defaults above are already set to this book, so you can check the arithmetic yourself.

Rules that reject covers. Spine text is printed only on books with more than 79 pages, and needs 0.0625 in (1.6 mm) of clear space on each side of the spine. Keep all front and back cover text at least 0.125 in inside the trim lines, keep the lower-right of the back cover clear for KDP’s barcode box, and re-run this calculator any time the page count changes — even by two pages.

One honest footnote: KDP’s own pages disagree in one small place. The Create a Paperback Cover page lists standard color at 0.002252 in per page (used here), while the Paperback Submission Guidelines list 0.002347 in for color books generally. For premium color both agree. If your book is standard color and the spine must be exact, confirm against KDP’s own cover template generator before you upload.

Frequently asked questions

What is the KDP spine width formula?

Spine width = page count × a per-page thickness set by your paper: 0.002252 in for black ink on white paper or standard color, 0.0025 in for cream, 0.00235 in for groundwood, and 0.002347 in for premium color. The full cover width is then 0.125 in bleed + trim width + spine + trim width + 0.125 in bleed.

How many pages do I need for spine text?

More than 79 pages — at 79 or fewer, KDP rejects a cover that carries spine text. KDP’s Cover Creator tool sets its own minimum at 80 pages. Spine text also needs 0.0625 in of clear space between the text and each edge of the spine.

Why did KDP reject my cover size?

The usual cause is a page-count change after the cover was designed: the spine is calculated from the final interior file, rounded up to an even page count. A two-page change moves the spine by about 0.005 in on white paper — small, but enough to fail the template check. Rebuild the cover from the final page count.

What resolution should a KDP cover file be?

300 DPI at the full cover size. This calculator shows the exact pixel dimensions: multiply each inch figure by 300. A 6 × 9 in, 200-page book on white paper needs a 3,810 × 2,775 px file.

Can I download a ready-made cover template?

Yes — the “Download print template” button above the FAQ generates a PNG at the exact full-cover pixel size for your trim, page count and paper, with the bleed boundary, trim lines, spine folds and text area, safe areas and the back-cover barcode box drawn and labelled. It is generated in your browser at 300 DPI. Use it as the bottom layer of your design and hide it before uploading; KDP also offers its own template generator in the Bookshelf, and the figures here follow the same official formulas.

Does this work for hardcover covers?

No — hardcover covers add case boards and hinge allowances on top of the paper stack, so the paperback formula does not apply. This calculator is paperback-only; hardcover printing costs and royalties are covered by the tools below.
