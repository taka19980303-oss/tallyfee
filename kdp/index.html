Two rule changes most calculators still get wrong

Print royalties are tiered now
Since June 2025, KDP print royalties are 60% at or above $9.99 (Amazon.com) and 50% below it — no longer a flat 60%. A book priced at $9.98 earns roughly a dollar less per sale than the same book at $9.99. Pages still showing a flat 60% overstate earnings on every sub-$9.99 book.
The eBook 70% band widened
Effective July 7, 2026, the 70% eBook royalty band on Amazon.com runs $2.99–$12.99 — the cap had been $9.99 since 2007. Books priced between $9.99 and $12.99 can now earn 70% instead of 35%. Equivalent changes apply in the other Kindle stores.

How a KDP book’s money flows

A print sale splits three ways: Amazon keeps its share of the list price, the printing cost comes out of your royalty, and the rest is yours — royalty = (rate × list price) − printing cost. The printing cost is why page count is a pricing decision, not just an editorial one: at black-ink rates, every 100 pages past the flat band adds $1.20 to the cost of each copy sold on Amazon.com. The cover runs on the same page count: spine width = pages × a paper factor, which is why the cover is the last file you can finish.
